# Your new website

Handmade Electric LLC, built by Align and Acquire.

This explains what you had, what you have now, and what it does for you. No
jargon. If a sentence in here does not make sense, ask and I will rewrite it.

---

## What the old site was doing to you

Your Squarespace site was not a website so much as a storefront that never
finished being set up. Three specific problems:

**Your services were listed as products in a shopping cart.** Residential,
commercial, and industrial were set up as store items, priced at $0.00, sitting
behind a checkout. A homeowner who wanted an electrician found something that
looked like an online shop with nothing for sale. There was a cart page on your
site. You cannot check out with an electrician.

**The links pointed at the wrong pages.** The "Residential" card sent people to
the industrial page. So the visitor most likely to hire you, a homeowner, got
sent to a page about manufacturing plants.

**The About page described a company that does not exist.** It talked about grid
performance, clients nationwide, and sustainable energy management. It read like
a national electric utility. It was template filler that came with the theme and
was never replaced. Anyone who clicked About learned nothing true about you, and
some of them concluded you were not a real local business.

None of that survives. Not a word of the About page was reused.

---

## What you have now

A twelve page website built from scratch, that loads fast on a phone, and that
says what you actually do.

**Pages people search for.** Instead of one page selling three products, there
is a page for each thing you do: residential, commercial, industrial, Generac
generator installation, generator repair and service, plus deeper pages on panel
replacement and lighting retrofits. Each page answers the questions people
actually type into Google before they call an electrician.

**The generator work leads.** You said standby generators are where you want to
grow, so the two generator pages got the most work in the whole project. They
explain how a standby system works, what a transfer switch does, what determines
what size unit a house needs, and why permits and inspection matter. Someone
researching a generator at 11pm can get real answers from your site instead of
from a competitor's.

**Your own photos, all through the site.** Twenty one job photos from your phone
are on the site now, including a Recent Work page showing all of them, sorted by
residential, commercial, and industrial. Real panels, real lighting jobs, real
conduit. Not stock photography of a model in a hard hat. That matters more than
most people think: it is the difference between looking like a real contractor
and looking like a lead broker.

**A quote form, alongside calling and texting.** Calling and texting are still on
every page, including a bar fixed to the bottom of the screen on phones. But a
lot of people will not call a stranger. They will fill out a form at 9pm. The
form asks what the job is, what kind of property, and the address or city, so you
know whether it is even in your range before you call back.

**Answers written into the pages.** Every service page has a question and answer
section. When someone asks Google or ChatGPT "why does my generator run once a
week" or "do I need a permit for a generator in Michigan", your site has a real
answer on the page. That is increasingly how people find contractors.

**Nothing invented.** This is the part I want to be direct about. Every factual
claim on the site traces back to something you told us or something that is
simply true of electrical work everywhere. There are no made up review counts, no
star ratings, no "30 minute response", no prices, and no claims about
certifications. If a fact was missing, the site says nothing rather than making
something up. That is why the list at the end of this document matters.

## What is deliberately not on the site yet

**No reviews.** You have not sent any, and I will not write fake ones. The review
section is built and switched off. Send real ones and it turns on in one change.

**No license number.** Your own logo says licensed electrical contractor, so I
believe you have one, but I do not have the number. Until I do, the site does not
use the word "licensed" anywhere, because a claim like that should be backed by a
number a homeowner can look up. This is the single most valuable thing you can
send me. It goes in the footer of every page.

**No hours, no street address.** Not confirmed, so not published.

**No specific cities.** The site says Metro Detroit, because that is what you
have confirmed. Once you tell me the towns you actually cover, the site can
target them specifically, which is how you show up when someone searches
"electrician in" their own town.

## The honest part about being found on Google

The website is the foundation, not the whole job. Two things matter more than
anything else on the site itself:

**You do not appear to have a Google Business Profile.** I could not find one.
That is the free Google listing with your map pin, your reviews, and your hours,
and it is what shows up when somebody searches "electrician near me". For a local
contractor it is usually worth more than the website. Creating and verifying it
should happen right after launch, and I will do it with you.

**Reviews on that profile.** Once the profile exists, asking every customer for a
review is the highest return activity available to you. Competitors who dominate
this market are not doing it with better websites. They are doing it with more
reviews.

The site is built to feed both of those. But they need to exist.
